# SmartSME — Video Scripts

Two films. Film 1 states the problem and never mentions the product. Film 2 answers
it. Shoot them so they can be watched back to back, or separately.

Voice-over pace assumed ~150 words/min. Word counts are given per film so you can
trim to the exact runtime.

---

# FILM 1 — "The Book"

**The problem, from the shopkeeper's side. No product, no logo until the last frame.**
**Runtime: ~1:45 · VO ~255 words**

---

**0:00–0:12 — Cold open**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | Pre-dawn. A shutter rattles up. RAMESH, 50s, flicks on a tube light in a kirana shop. Tight on his hands untying a cloth-bound ledger, spine cracked, pages swollen. |
| **VO** | Ramesh has run this shop for nineteen years. Everything it has ever earned is in this book. |

**0:12–0:32 — The day begins**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | Fast cuts, handheld, no music yet: a customer points at a shelf; Ramesh scribbles on a paper slip; a phone buzzes face-down on the counter; he wipes his hands, picks it up, reads an order, puts it down; a child hands him a folded note from her mother. |
| **VO** | An order comes on the phone. One comes on a scrap of paper. One comes shouted from the door while he is weighing rice for somebody else. He remembers all of it. Most of it. |

**0:32–0:52 — The gap opens**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | A customer asks for something. Ramesh goes to the back. Beat. He comes out empty-handed and shrugs. The customer leaves. Hold on the empty doorway. |
| **VO** | He did not know it had run out. Nothing told him. The book records what he sold — it has never once warned him what he is about to be unable to sell. |

**0:52–1:12 — Udhaar**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | Night. Shutter half down. Ramesh under a single bulb, ledger open, running a finger down a column of names, lips moving. He flips back a page. Flips forward again. Stops. |
| **VO** | Eleven people owe him money. He is fairly sure it is eleven. To find out how much, he will read four months backwards, and he will still be guessing about Tuesday. |

**1:12–1:32 — Why not software**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | Cut to a dusty laptop on a shelf above the rice sacks, closed. Then a phone screen: a business app's sign-up form — a long English form, twelve empty fields, a red asterisk beside each one. Ramesh's thumb hovers. He closes it. |
| **VO** | He has tried. The software wanted him to type in English. It wanted a product code, a tax slab, a customer ID — twelve fields for something he could say in six words. It was built for a company with a data entry clerk. He is the clerk, the cashier, and the man carrying the sacks. |

**1:32–1:45 — Turn**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | Back to the ledger. Ramesh's hand flat on the open page. Slow push in. Cut to black a beat early. |
| **VO** | The problem was never that Ramesh will not keep records. He keeps them obsessively. The problem is that every system built to help him asked him to become someone else first. |
| **Title card** | *What if it just understood how he already works?* |

# FILM 2 — "It Already Understands"

**The answer. Every capability shown here exists in the build — nothing aspirational.**
**Runtime: ~2:00 · VO ~295 words**

---

**0:00–0:10 — Pick up where Film 1 ended**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | Same shop, same counter, morning. The ledger is still there, closed, pushed to one side. A phone sits where it was. |
| **VO** | Nothing about the shop has to change. Not the book, not the slips, not the way he talks to people. |

**0:10–0:35 — Say it the way you'd say it**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | Screen recording, full-bleed, large type. Typed live, one after another, each producing the *same* draft card: `sold 5 kg rice to Anita Stores` → `Anita ko 5 kilo chawal becha` → `अनीता को 5 किलो चावल बेचा` → `ಅನಿತಾಗೆ ೫ ಕಿಲೋ ಅಕ್ಕಿ ಮಾರಿದೆ`. Each time the card fills in: **Anita Stores · Rice Bag 25kg · 5 · ₹—**. |
| **VO** | He writes it the way he would say it. English, Hindi, Kannada — typed in the script or typed in Latin letters, the way a phone keyboard actually gets used. It reads all of them, and it knows that चावल and ಅಕ್ಕಿ and "chawal" are the rice on his own shelf, at his own price. |

**0:35–0:58 — Or don't type at all**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | A hand photographs a handwritten order slip on the counter. Push in on the paper: one line has an item scored out and rewritten. On screen the draft appears with that cancelled line **absent**, the corrected one present. |
| **VO** | Or he photographs the slip the customer handed him. Including the line she crossed out and wrote again — which a plain scanner would happily bill him for twice. |

**0:58–1:18 — Orders that arrive by themselves**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | Split screen: left, a customer sends an order as a message; right, the shop's Inbox page — a new draft appears in the queue, customer and product already matched. Ramesh taps **Accept**. |
| **VO** | Orders sent in by message or by email arrive as drafts on their own, with the customer and the item already matched to his catalogue. He accepts, or he doesn't. |

**1:18–1:32 — The line that earns trust**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | Slow, deliberate. The confirm screen, full frame. Cursor rests on **Confirm**. Hold two full seconds before the tap. |
| **VO** | And nothing — nothing — is ever written to his books until he looks at it and says yes. |

**1:32–1:50 — What happens after the tap**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | One continuous take after the tap: the sale appears; cut to Products — the rice count has already dropped; cut to the customer's balance — updated; a low-stock alert slides in. Then the Dashboard: today's sales, what's owed to him, what he owes. |
| **VO** | One tap, and the stock has already moved. Her balance is updated. And because the sugar just fell below where it should be, he is told now — standing here — instead of finding out from a customer walking away empty-handed. |

**1:50–2:00 — Close**

| | |
|---|---|
| **On screen** | Wide. Ramesh serving a customer, phone face-down on the counter, not in his hand. The ledger still sits closed beside the till. Logo, then URL. |
| **VO** | He still runs the shop the way he always has. The books just keep themselves now. |
| **Title card** | **SmartSME** — *your shop, in your words.* |

## Production notes

**Claim discipline.** Everything in Film 2 is something the build actually does:
multilingual typed notes across four variants, catalogue matching across scripts,
photographed slips with strikethrough handling, inbound orders as drafts, the
mandatory confirm step, inventory and party balances moving on the event chain,
low-stock alerts, and the dashboard. Do **not** add voice input, WhatsApp as a live
channel, or any accuracy percentage to the narration — none of those are true today.
The OCR demo should be shot against a real slip from `backend/tests/fixtures`, not a
prop, because the strikethrough beat only lands if it is genuine.

**Casting and language.** Ramesh should speak nothing on camera in either film; the
VO carries it. That keeps one edit usable for a Hindi or Kannada VO track later
without a reshoot — worth doing, given the audience.

**The strikethrough shot is the money shot.** It is the one moment a viewer sees
something they did not know was a problem. Give it the extra two seconds; cut
elsewhere if you need the time.

**Pacing.** Film 1 should feel slightly too long and slightly airless — that is the
point. Film 2 should be the opposite: quick, clean, and over before it outstays
itself. Resist scoring Film 1 until 0:32.
